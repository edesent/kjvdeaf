# Editorial Guidelines — KJV Deaf Bible

Standing rules applied throughout this project. Keep this file updated as new standards are established.

## Stay faithful to the KJV (TOP RULE -- overrides every other rule)
The KJV is the standard. Simplifying can change the words, but never what the verse says.
- **Same subject.** Who does the action stays the same. "By Him were all things created" (Colossians 1:16) means the Son created -- not "God made everything through the Son."
- **Same action.** What happens stays the same. "Took on Him the seed of Abraham" (Hebrews 2:16) means Jesus became human -- not "Jesus helps."
- **Same condition.** Every "if," "unless," and command stays. "If ye keep in memory" (1 Corinthians 15:2) stays a condition.
- **No added explanation.** Do not add meaning the KJV does not have (e.g. "without complete commitment").
- **Follow the KJV's Greek text (Received Text), not modern Bibles.** "The prophets" in Mark 1:2; "God was manifest in the flesh" in 1 Timothy 3:16.
- **The KJV wins.** Hebrew/Greek is used to understand KJV words, not to replace them. If a modern reading of the Hebrew or Greek seems to differ from the KJV, keep the KJV (e.g. Proverbs 25:23 "the north wind drives away rain"; Psalm 139:16 "all my body parts").
- **Do not copy other Bibles.** No wording taken from the Easy-to-Read Version, Amplified Bible, NIV, or similar.
- **Reading level: about 5th grade, Deaf-friendly -- but as close to the KJV as that allows.** Keep the KJV's order of ideas and its key words, names, and titles (e.g. "firstborn," "Gentiles," "submit," "Barbarian, Scythian"). If a KJV word is too hard, use the simplest word that means exactly the same thing, or keep the word and explain it briefly -- do not swap in a looser idea.
- **Source check for the Old Testament:** the KJV follows the Ben Chayyim Masoretic text (1524-25). Never follow modern Bibles where they change the Hebrew using the Septuagint, Dead Sea Scrolls, Samaritan, Syriac, or guesses (often marked in their footnotes). Known places where the KJV's Hebrew differs from modern Bibles' Hebrew: 1 Kings 20:38, Proverbs 8:16, Isaiah 10:16, Isaiah 27:2, Isaiah 38:14, Ezekiel 30:18, Zephaniah 3:15, Malachi 1:12 -- always follow the KJV there.
- **Where the English word and the ASL meaning do not agree, go back to the original.** Many KJV words meant something different in 1611, or read differently to a Deaf signer than to a hearing reader. Use the Hebrew/Greek to find what the KJV actually means, and write that meaning plainly -- e.g. "conversation" = way of life, "suffer" = allow, "prevent" = go ahead of, "want" = lack, "spoil" = war treasure, "bowels" = deep love/tender feelings. Avoid English idioms that do not carry into ASL. The original language is used to make the KJV's meaning clear, never to replace it with a modern Bible's reading.
- **Hard words: keep the KJV word and explain it in parentheses.** Example: "a strong delusion (a powerful lie)," "quench (put out) the Spirit," "His saints (holy people)," "repentance (a change of heart)." Keep the explanation short and simple, and use the same explanation for the same word everywhere. **Explain it every time the word appears** (not only the first time).

- **Genesis 3:15:** the woman's seed is written "His heel" (capitalized), because the verse points to Christ.
- **Names: keep the familiar form.** Where the KJV uses another form of the same person's name (Silvanus/Silas, Marcus/Mark, Lucas/Luke, Prisca/Priscilla, Timotheus/Timothy), use the familiar name Deaf readers know from the rest of the Bible. Spelling-only differences (e.g. Phygellus) follow the KJV.
- **Still keep short sentences and clear pronouns.** Staying close to the KJV never cancels the Deaf-first rules: break long KJV sentences into short ones (aim for 8-14 words), and name the person instead of a pronoun whenever "he/him/they/it" could be unclear (see Pronoun clarity below).
- **Source check for the New Testament:** Jack Moorman, *8,000 Differences Between the Textus Receptus and the Nestle-Aland NT Greek Texts* -- every reading must follow the Textus Receptus column.

## Core approach
- **KJV base text**; Hebrew/Greek (Strong's Concordance / Blue Letter Bible) used only to understand the KJV's words
- Plain, direct, everyday English — avoid elevated/Latinate vocabulary (e.g. "transfigured," "customs duties," "exempt," "luminous") even when it would be more "literary." Sharpen concrete/visual details when they carry real meaning (e.g. "torn open" vs "open," "dug through" vs "made a hole"), but don't add exposition the original text doesn't have.

## Pronoun clarity — PREFER EXPLICIT NOUNS OVER PRONOUNS
This is a strong standing preference, not just a fallback for genuine ambiguity. Default to naming the person/subject explicitly (e.g. "Jesus said" rather than "He said") even in cases where the pronoun's referent is technically clear from context. Apply this more liberally than a strict "fix only real ambiguity" rule — err on the side of naming.

Exceptions where pronouns are fine as-is:
- Single, unambiguous fictional character within one parable/story (e.g. "a man" who plants seed — his own "he/his" throughout that story)
- First-person "I" (never ambiguous)
- Very short-range continuation within the same sentence/clause where renaming would feel unnatural or overly repetitive (judgment call — when in doubt, use the noun)

Also watch for **over-repetition** in the other direction: if a name is repeated many times in quick succession with no ambiguity risk, smooth some instances back to a pronoun (e.g. "Mary Magdalene and the other Mary" repeated 6x in Matthew 28 was trimmed to "they/them" after first establishing both women).

## Divine pronoun capitalization (REVERENTIAL CAPITALIZATION -- updated standard)
Capitalize all pronouns referring to God the Father, Jesus Christ, and the Holy Spirit: **He, Him, His, Himself, You, Your, Yours, Thy, Thine** (when directly addressing God/Jesus in prayer or speech). This matches the convention used in NKJV and NASB.

This is a reversal of an earlier standard used in parts of this project (which called for lowercase divine pronouns to match modern translations like NIV). As of this update, capitalization is the correct standard going forward, and a retroactive fix is in progress across books where the old lowercase standard was applied (Matthew, Mark, Luke, John chapters 2-6, 1 John 4). Check a book/chapter's status before assuming which standard was applied -- if in doubt, check for consistency within the chapter itself and fix to capitalized.

Do NOT capitalize pronouns referring to ordinary people (Peter, Pilate, Herod, "a man," etc.) -- this rule applies only to references to Deity.

## LORD / Lord / lord
- **LORD** (all caps): the divine name YHWH, almost exclusively in Old Testament quotations (e.g. "The LORD said to my Lord," Psalm 110:1, quoted in Matthew 22:44).
- **Lord** (capital L only): a title for God or Jesus (Greek *Kyrios*). This is the normal NT usage -- "the Lord Jesus," "Lord, have mercy on me," etc.
- **lord** (lowercase): an ordinary human master or authority -- a servant's master in a parable, or addressing a king/ruler as "my lord." Not a divine reference.

## Gender-neutral language
Convert generic "he/his/him" (referring to "anyone," "whoever," "a person," "a prophet," etc.) to gender-neutral phrasing ("that person," "they/their," or restructuring). Exception: gendered passages that are textually specific (e.g. husband/wife qualifications, "husband of one wife," household codes) stay gendered.

## Capitalization
Every verse must start with a capital letter, including continuation verses that begin mid-sentence in the KJV (e.g. "and," "who," "for").

## Hell vs. the grave (updated -- follow the KJV word)
- Hebrew *sheol* / Greek *Hades* → use whatever word the KJV uses in that verse: where the KJV says "hell," write **hell** with no parentheses (e.g. Matthew 11:23 "brought down to hell"); where it says "grave" or "pit," keep that word.
- Greek *Gehenna* (NT judgment/fire imagery) → "hell" (kept as-is)
- 2 Peter 2:4's *Tartarus* → "hell" (kept as-is)
- (This replaces the earlier rule that changed every *sheol*/*Hades* to "the grave.")

## Word-level consistency across the whole book
When a specific term matters (e.g. the Greek uses two different words for "baskets" — *kophinoi*, small hand-baskets, at the feeding of the 5,000, vs. *spyrides*, large provision hampers, at the feeding of the 4,000), preserve that same distinction consistently everywhere it appears, including in parallel passages across different books (Matthew/Mark) and in places where Jesus himself references both events (Matthew 16:9-10, Mark 8:19-20).

## No contractions
Formal, direct phrasing — no contractions in the text itself.

## Never use "deaf" as a negative metaphor
E.g. "turns a deaf ear" → "refuses to listen."
